# Closure and open promises

Use this reference at architecture, midpoint, ending design, and pre-lock. Closure is not the same as explaining everything. It is the deliberate treatment of the expectations the book created.

## Classify the promise

Track each material thread by type:

- **plot obligation:** an external task, danger, decision, or contest;
- **mystery:** a factual question or pattern the reader is invited to interpret;
- **character debt:** a choice, harm, lie, need, or responsibility that calls for reckoning;
- **relationship:** a bond whose terms must change, break, or be consciously carried;
- **thematic question:** an inquiry the book embodies rather than proves;
- **world consequence:** the social, political, ecological, or material aftereffect of action;
- **motif or formal promise:** an image, pattern, document form, rhythm, or structural device whose recurrence creates expectation.

Also record how strongly the book made the promise—explicit, strongly implied, or reader-inferred—and its intended scope: scene, chapter, volume, or series.

## Choose one treatment

- **Answer:** provide the fact, result, or confrontation the contract requires, then show its consequence.
- **Transform:** the original question becomes a deeper or different question because of earned recognition or action.
- **Leave deliberately open:** preserve live interpretations while completing the emotional, relational, thematic, or formal movement that made uncertainty meaningful.
- **Defer:** carry the promise to a later volume with a current-volume consequence, changed terms, and a clear reason the answer is not due yet.

Do not use `open` or `defer` as a label for forgotten setup. Do not answer a thematic question as a slogan. Do not solve a mystery whose uncertainty is part of the stated reader experience unless the author reopens that decision.

## Apply closure gates

Before approving an ending, check:

1. Every explicit volume-level promise has a recorded treatment.
2. Any transformed promise has an on-page choice, recognition, or consequence that performs the transformation.
3. Any deliberately open mystery has bounded interpretations and a completed non-factual movement; it is not arbitrary.
4. Any series deferral changes the current book and does not make the volume disposable.
5. Character debts and world consequences are not erased by a plot victory.
6. Motifs and formal devices culminate, alter, or fall silent deliberately rather than receiving a forced decoding.
7. The final image, action, or rhythm grows from prior setup instead of arriving as an editorial explanation.

Audit the ledger at the midpoint and before lock. When an ending decision changes, propagate it backward into setup and forward into world, character, and series state.
