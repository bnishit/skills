# Ensemble, epic, and long-series fiction

Use this reference when several viewpoint characters, factions, locations, or volumes create coupled continuity and payoff risk.

## Set the scale contract

State the scope of the current volume and the wider series separately. Name the main collective pressure, the volume's irreversible change, the intended convergence, and what a reader should receive before this volume ends.

Budget viewpoints. Each viewpoint must provide at least one thing no other viewpoint can: causal power, information access, moral angle, emotional stake, or lived region of the world. Do not add a viewpoint only to witness exposition. A viewpoint may be unequal in page count while still carrying a complete local movement.

## Keep viewpoints distinct

Track for each viewpoint:

- external objective and private stake;
- faction ties, leverage, and constraints;
- knowledge, false beliefs, and secrets;
- arc state and next irreversible choice;
- perceptual bias, values, syntax, cadence, humor, and avoided subjects;
- last appearance, elapsed story time, location, and next dependency.

Differentiate voices through what a person notices, values, compares, and refuses—not spelling tricks, catchphrases, or caricatured dialect. Before scaling, prototype two viewpoints observing the same event without names and test whether the distinction survives.

## Make politics causal

Treat a faction as people using institutions, resources, stories, and force under constraint. For every major political turn, track:

    prior condition -> actor's belief -> decision -> mechanism -> affected groups -> reaction -> new constraint

Record legitimacy, material resources, internal splits, public story, private objective, alliances, and red lines. A decree, betrayal, battle, shortage, or revolt must propagate through incentives and communication; it cannot happen only because the outline needs scale.

## Synchronize time, geography, and information

Keep one canonical event timeline. Every scene records start and end time, location, viewpoint, public events, private actions, and the earliest later scenes it can affect.

Maintain a travel and communication table with route, season, mode, ordinary duration, blocked conditions, and uncertainty. Derive arrivals from constraints rather than moving characters at plot speed. When facts change, propagate them through journeys, witnesses, messages, rumors, and institutional delay.

Use a reveal ledger for the underlying fact, who knows it, what evidence exists, what the reader knows, likely false inference, decision changed by the reveal, and intended reveal window. Prevent a viewpoint from borrowing author knowledge. Let delayed or distorted information cause action when the world supports that delay.

## Converge causally

Plan convergence backward from prerequisites. For each strand, name the decision, resource, message, loss, or route that makes arrival possible and the cost of joining the shared event. Characters should not appear at the climax because the author summoned the cast.

Keep setup and payoff visible at three scales: scene, volume, and series. A later-volume setup must still affect the current volume through pressure, consequence, or changed understanding. Do not make a whole book feel like prologue.

## Revise the coupled system

Run separate audits for:

1. each viewpoint read alone as an arc;
2. the canonical chronology and travel feasibility;
3. faction cause and consequence;
4. knowledge and reveal timing;
5. setup, payoff, and convergence;
6. volume closure versus series deferral;
7. canon propagation after late changes.

Use [fiction-closure.md](fiction-closure.md) before ending a volume. A series can defer its largest answer while the current book still completes a meaningful arc and makes the cost of deferral visible.
