# Personal and reflective books

Use this mode when the book's value comes from the author's lived experience, private reasoning, relationships, or emotional truth.

## Interview for discovery

Start at the root rather than collecting polished declarations. Ask about a concrete memory, choice, fear, or ordinary day. Follow the author's language:

- What happened before this belief existed?
- What did the author do, notice, avoid, or change?
- What would make the claim untrue?
- Which part is chosen, and which part is circumstantial?
- What would the intended reader reasonably doubt?

Ask one question at a time unless the author requests a batch. A good next question arises from the last answer; it does not merely advance a questionnaire.

Keep a raw-answer ledger. Label editorial conclusions as conclusions. When the author corrects an interpretation, update the working model rather than preserving both as false balance.

## Build the arc

Structure around movement in understanding, not a list of topics. Common movements include old belief to new belief, distance to recognition, fear to chosen responsibility, or a question examined from increasingly costly angles. These are options, not a required formula.

Use concrete ordinary moments as evidence. Keep tender material specific enough to feel true but never include sensitive events merely because they are dramatic. If the author names a boundary, preserve the insight while removing the identifying event.

## Voice and consent

The intended reader may know the author well. Read aloud for phrases the author would never say, grand promises unsupported by behavior, therapy-speak, sales language, and sacrifice accounting.

Distinguish:

- what the author feels;
- what the author has observed about another person;
- what the other person explicitly said;
- what the author hopes will be true.

Do not use private messages from third parties without permission. Paraphrase or omit details whose disclosure would betray someone else's confidence. The author's commitment to openness does not transfer ownership of another person's secrets.

Before lock, run a recipient reading: likely question, passage that answers it, remaining ambiguity, and whether the answer sounds lived rather than litigated.
