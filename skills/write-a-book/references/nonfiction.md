# Nonfiction books

Use this mode for explanation, argument, reporting, history, research, or practical teaching.

## Define the intellectual promise

State the central question, the proposed answer, the intended reader, and what the reader should understand or be able to do afterward. Identify the strongest plausible rival explanation before committing to the outline.

Build a claim ledger with:

- claim and chapter;
- importance to the thesis;
- source or evidence type;
- confidence and material limitations;
- counterargument or alternative explanation;
- citation and verification status.

Research can change the thesis. Do not use sources merely to decorate a conclusion chosen in advance.

If a personal Shelf, bookmarks connector, or saved-reading corpus is available, search it for candidate sources and the author's prior questions. Treat saves as bookmarks, not as works the author has read, endorsed, or understood. Verify consequential claims against the underlying source before using them.

## Structure the argument

Each chapter should answer a necessary question raised by the prior chapter. Distinguish background, evidence, interpretation, example, and recommendation. Use narrative scenes only when sourced; never invent a composite person or dialogue without disclosure.

For advice, separate mechanism from anecdote and observation from causal claim. Mark current facts that need live verification. Prefer primary sources for consequential or technical claims.

## Revise for trust

Run a claim audit before line editing:

- Does the evidence support the strength and scope of the sentence?
- Are selection effects, uncertainty, and disagreement visible where material?
- Does a vivid example improperly stand in for prevalence?
- Could the reader locate the source behind a consequential claim?
- Did a late research finding make an earlier chapter misleading?

Then test pedagogy: terms introduced before use, examples that clarify rather than distract, summaries that advance rather than repeat, and exercises that genuinely test the chapter's idea.
