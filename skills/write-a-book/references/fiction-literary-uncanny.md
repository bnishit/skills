# Literary, uncanny, intimate, and formally unusual fiction

Use this reference when the book's movement depends substantially on interiority, recurrence, silence, dream logic, ambiguity, or formal variation rather than a conventional plot spine.

## Build a pressure system

Define five anchors before outlining:

- the ordinary practice that keeps the character in contact with the world;
- the relationship, absence, contradiction, or question exerting pressure;
- the intrusion or recurring disturbance;
- the formal device through which the reader experiences it;
- the emotional or thematic movement the ending may complete without solving the disturbance.

Do not diagnose the story into a single hidden cause too early. Keep two or three live readings when the user wants ambiguity, and record what evidence each reading can and cannot explain.

## Give dream logic rules without explaining the dream

Dream logic is not randomness. Choose a small set of felt operations such as association, repetition with variation, substitution, contamination, omission, doubling, or impossible adjacency. Track their recurrence and escalation. The reader may never receive a cosmological answer, but should learn how to notice the pattern.

Keep ordinary anchors—work, appetite, money, weather, objects, other people—so the strange has something to disturb. Distinguish deliberate indeterminacy from accidental confusion: the reader may be unsure what an event means without being unsure whose scene it is, what changed, or why it matters now.

## Use negative space deliberately

For each important omission, record:

- what is absent or withheld;
- who cannot or will not say it;
- what pressure the absence creates;
- what false reading it risks;
- whether it will be answered, transformed, revisited, or kept open.

Do not withhold basic orientation merely to manufacture mystery. Silence must alter the reader's relation to what is present.

## Track interior and formal movement

A quiet scene or fragment earns its place when it changes attention, self-deception, intimacy, memory, shame, desire, an image's meaning, the reliability of a form, or the rhythm through which experience is perceived. Name that change in the ledger. Do not add an arbitrary confrontation just to make the scene look active.

Design recurring images as a system, not a code. Let an image accrue, migrate, collide with another image, or reverse its emotional charge. Do not force every recurrence to symbolize one thing, and do not decode the system for the reader at the end.

Create a voice score for each sustained narrative register: distance, syntax, cadence, sensory bias, figurative habits, humor, and characteristic silence. Prototype the same charged moment in two rhythms before choosing one. Read aloud for breath, deliberate repetition, and unearned lyricism.

## Handle trauma without turning it into spectacle

- Give the character a life larger than what happened: skills, appetites, work, loyalties, irritation, play, and future-facing choices.
- Use only the detail needed for the scene's human or thematic work. Aftermath, adaptation, relationships, and consequence often matter more than graphic reconstruction.
- Do not use a diagnosis as destiny, make pain the automatic source of special insight, or treat disclosure as the only proof of intimacy.
- When real experience informs the fiction, preserve the source and permission boundary from the project file. Fictionalization does not erase ownership or privacy risk.
- Run a necessity check on intense material: whose experience is centered, what changes because it is shown, what a less explicit version would lose, and whether the scene invites understanding or merely shock.

## Earn an open ending

Read [fiction-closure.md](fiction-closure.md). Separate factual explanation from emotional, relational, thematic, and formal completion. An unexplained phenomenon can remain open when the ending changes the character's relation to it, culminates the image or formal system, and honors the strongest reader promises made by the book.

Do not add a final explanation merely to make the ending legible. Do not call an ending ambiguous when it has simply abandoned consequences.
