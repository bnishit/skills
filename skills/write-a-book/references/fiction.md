# Fiction books

Use this mode when the book's truth is carried by invented characters, scenes, consequences, images, voice, or form.

If the user names a living writer as a style target, refuse direct imitation briefly and translate the request into permitted high-level craft dimensions. State the resulting craft brief in one sentence before the prototype so the boundary is observable. Offer an original combination of narrative distance, rhythm, imagery, humor, structure, revelation, or thematic pressure. Do not echo signature phrasing, characters, worlds, or recognizable scene patterns. Do not flatten fiction into short, uniformly simple sentences merely because that register suits briefs or reports.

## Choose the architecture

Name the working architecture before outlining. It is a decision aid, not a genre label.

- **Dramatic or causal:** choices and consequences supply most of the forward movement. Use the causal chain below.
- **Literary, uncanny, intimate, or formally unusual:** emotional pressure, perception, recurrence, omission, and form may carry as much movement as plot. Read [fiction-literary-uncanny.md](fiction-literary-uncanny.md) and use [the literary state asset](../assets/fiction-literary-state.md).
- **Ensemble, epic, or long-series:** multiple viewpoints and institutions create scale and continuity risk. Read [fiction-ensemble-epic.md](fiction-ensemble-epic.md) and use [the ensemble state asset](../assets/fiction-ensemble-state.md).
- **Hybrid:** choose the lead architecture, load both relevant references, and state which rules govern which strand. Do not make one architecture erase the other.

Use [assets/fiction-bible.md](../assets/fiction-bible.md) for shared canon. When planning an ending, revising late chapters, or carrying threads across volumes, also read [fiction-closure.md](fiction-closure.md).

## Establish the story engine

Write a story promise that names the intended experience, organizing pressure, central characters or collective, constraint, destabilizing change, and ending direction. A single protagonist with a clear external desire is one option, not a requirement.

Generate alternatives when the premise is still open. Vary the underlying engine—not just names, settings, or endings. Compare each option by emotional pressure, causal or associative richness, character cost, freshness, formal fit, and ability to sustain a book. Recommend one without treating unchosen details as canon.

## Create movement that fits the mode

For dramatic or causal work, build from decisions and consequences:

    pressure -> choice -> result -> changed options -> greater pressure

Coincidence may create trouble; it should not repeatedly solve it. The protagonist's special advantage must meet a credible constraint. Knowledge without proof, power without legitimacy, or foresight without control can create stronger drama than arbitrary obstacles.

Across all modes, each scene or fragment must alter at least one live dimension: options, knowledge, relationship, commitment, emotional pressure, interpretive frame, image pattern, temporal relation, or voice. Atmosphere can be movement when its recurrence changes meaning; undirected repetition is not.

For speculative, alternate-history, mystery, or time-travel premises, maintain timeline and knowledge state. Track who knows what, when they could know it, what evidence they can show, which intervention changes later conditions, and the unintended effects of success. Research the period without turning the novel into a tactical manual or letting exposition replace human stakes.

## Build character under pressure

Give major characters a public strategy, private need, defended belief or wound, leverage, contradiction, and boundary they may cross. Do not force every character into a neat healing arc. Reveal them through attention, avoidance, relationship, language, and costly choices rather than biography delivered in dialogue.

Opposition should have an intelligible model of the world, not exist only to delay the plot. Preserve ordinary wants, competence, humor, and agency around grief or trauma; suffering is not a complete identity.

## Draft and revise

Use scene cards proportionate to the mode. A causal scene may need viewpoint, immediate want, opposition, turn, cost, and exit condition. A literary fragment may instead need pressure, image or formal move, what is withheld, rhythm, and changed interpretation. Both need continuity dependencies.

Revise in this order:

1. promise, architecture, and ending alignment;
2. mode-specific movement and pressure;
3. character and relationship change;
4. timeline, knowledge, world, clue, motif, and promise continuity;
5. scene or fragment purpose, pace, and point of view;
6. prose, dialogue, image systems, silence, and read-aloud rhythm.

Do not preserve a beautiful passage that breaks the book's chosen logic. Do not make every chapter peak at the same intensity. Leave room for consequence, intimacy, humor, anticipation, and silence.
