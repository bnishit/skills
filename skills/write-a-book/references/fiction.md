# Fiction books

Use this mode when the book's truth is carried by invented characters, scenes, and consequences.

If the user names a living writer as a style target, refuse direct imitation briefly and translate the request into permitted high-level traits. Offer an original combination of narrative distance, rhythm, imagery, humor, structure, or thematic pressure. Do not flatten fiction into short, uniformly simple sentences merely because that register suits briefs or reports.

## Establish the story engine

Write a story promise that names the protagonist, desire, constraint, destabilizing change, central pressure, and experience promised to the reader. Use [assets/fiction-bible.md](../assets/fiction-bible.md) for durable state.

Generate alternatives when the premise is still open. Vary the underlying engine—not just names, settings, or endings. Compare each option by emotional pressure, causal richness, character cost, freshness, and ability to sustain a book. Recommend one without treating it as canon until chosen.

## Make plot causal

Build from decisions and consequences:

    pressure -> choice -> result -> changed options -> greater pressure

A scene must change knowledge, relationship, capability, risk, commitment, or available choices. Atmosphere alone is not movement. Coincidence may create trouble; it should not repeatedly solve it.

For speculative, alternate-history, mystery, or time-travel premises, maintain a timeline and knowledge ledger. Track who knows what, when they could know it, what evidence they can show, which intervention changes later conditions, and the unintended effects of each success. Research the period without turning the novel into a tactical manual or letting exposition replace human stakes.

## Build character under pressure

Give major characters an external want, private need, false belief or defended wound, leverage, contradiction, and boundary they may cross. Reveal character through costly choices, not biographies delivered in dialogue.

The protagonist's special advantage must meet a credible constraint. Knowledge without proof, power without legitimacy, or foresight without control can create stronger drama than arbitrary obstacles. Opposition should have an intelligible model of the world, not exist only to delay the plot.

## Draft and revise

Use scene cards with viewpoint, immediate want, opposition, turn, cost, exit condition, and continuity dependencies. Draft scenes for lived experience first; repair explanation on the next pass.

Revise in this order:

1. premise and ending alignment;
2. causal chain and escalation;
3. character arc and relationship change;
4. timeline, knowledge, world, and clue continuity;
5. scene purpose, pace, and point of view;
6. prose, dialogue, image systems, and read-aloud rhythm.

Do not preserve a beautiful scene that breaks the story's causality. Do not make every chapter peak at the same intensity. Leave room for consequence, intimacy, humor, and anticipation.
