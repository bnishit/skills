# Art direction

## Write the visual sentence first

Describe the desired experience in one sentence: reader, feeling, material, and restraint. Example pattern:

> A quiet field notebook for a curious night reader: warm paper, ink lines, one playful interruption per chapter, never sugary.

Use that sentence to reject attractive images that belong to a different book.

Then run the substitution test: if the same cover, sticker, palette, or marginalia could move to an unrelated book after changing only the title, it is too generic. Warm paper, serif type, plants, soft gradients, and other familiar signals are not a point of view by themselves. Every recurring choice should reveal something specific about this reader, this manuscript, or the way the book wants to be read.

## Build a small system

Choose before generating:

- one primary and one accent color, plus paper and ink neutrals
- display face, reading face, and utility face roles
- image medium and edge treatment
- recurring motif with a reason to return
- cover composition and thumbnail hierarchy
- maximum decorative density per chapter

Create a contact sheet and choose the system before producing the full set. Keep prompts free of final title text; typeset title and author separately.

For every motif, finish the sentence: “This belongs in this book because …” Reject a decorative answer such as “it feels literary” or “it looks warm.” Prefer a source-backed reason: a repeated object, shared joke, setting, ritual, narrative mechanism, or emotional counterpoint. Specificity matters more than fashionable polish.

## Illustration brief

For each image record:

- chapter and exact placement
- reader purpose: orient, deepen, breathe, recall, or delight
- visible subjects and action
- emotional temperature
- palette, medium, crop, and negative space
- details that must not appear
- alt text or `decorative`

Reject an image when it contradicts the prose, changes a person's identity, exposes private detail, contains garbled lettering, or merely repeats the adjacent paragraph.

## Personal references

Use only details present in the approved source or explicitly supplied for production. A private reference should feel like recognition to its reader, not an explanation to outsiders. Do not invent intimacy, reconstruct real faces without permission, or turn sensitive events into decoration.

## Rights ledger

For every image and font, retain source, creator/model, license or permission, generation date, and allowed editions. Exclude anything with unclear rights from the release.
