# Research and evidence

Research should change a decision in the skill. Do not collect an impressive reading list that never affects routing, constraints, or tests.

## Source order

Prefer:

1. official specifications and product documentation;
2. primary research or firsthand technical accounts;
3. maintained, reputable secondary synthesis;
4. practitioner reports, posts, saved links, and examples as hypotheses to test.

Record the source date or version when guidance can drift. For technical work, prefer the source that defines the behavior over a tutorial describing it.

## Internal knowledge and Shelf-like stores

Search relevant internal material when available, but distinguish:

- **saved:** the user kept the item;
- **read:** the user actually engaged with it;
- **endorsed:** the user agrees with it;
- **verified:** its underlying claim survived checking.

A saved item establishes only the first state unless other evidence says more. Use it to recover vocabulary, prior questions, examples, and candidate methods. Verify consequential claims against their underlying source or through an eval.

Never expose secrets, private annotations, or unrelated saved material in a portable skill. Summarize the reusable principle and retain provenance in the private research record.

## Convert reading into a decision

For each retained insight, write:

- the observed problem;
- the proposed skill change;
- the eval case that could falsify the change;
- the cost or new failure mode it may introduce.

If there is no falsifiable effect, it belongs in background notes rather than the skill.

## Useful research themes

- progressive disclosure and precise descriptions for routing;
- deterministic code for repeated fragile mechanics;
- realistic evaluation from actual failures;
- accessibility, privacy, and security standards for produced artifacts;
- domain-specific craft only where the base agent lacks a crucial decision rule.
